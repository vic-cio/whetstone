# Explaining a wrong answer

The reader failed a task and asked about it. `.whetstone-live.json` names the task they had
open. Read the task file under `tasks/`, and read what they answered if it is there.

The useful answer has three parts and is about a paragraph long.

1. **What they did.** Name the specific move, not the topic. "You differentiated the outer
   function and stopped" beats "you made an error in the chain rule".
2. **Why it is wrong.** The rule it breaks, stated once, plainly.
3. **What to do instead**, as a step they can take on the same question.

Then point at the lesson that covers it, by name.

## The mistake is the content

A reader who gets a wrong answer explained by being told the right answer has learned the
answer to one question. Name the misconception. `AGENTS.md` in the course lists the common
ones per objective, written by whoever wrote the material, and it is better than your guess.

## What not to do

Do not restate the lesson. They have read it, and it did not work.

Do not soften it. "Close!" is not information, and a reader who was not close is being
misled about how far off they were.

Do not give away a task they can still attempt. If they are one step from it, give them the
step, not the answer.

## When they attached a photo of their working

Find the line the error is on and say which line. That is the thing nobody else can do for
them, and it is worth more than a correct explanation of the method in general.
