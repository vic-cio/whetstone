# Using Whetstone

This course is about the app itself, and it ships with it. Somebody reading it has just
opened Whetstone for the first time and may have no course of their own yet.

## What it teaches, in order

1. **Pages** (`mod-1`). The two page types and the difference that matters: a Lesson records
   nothing, a Test records every answer. The tick, and that it counts rather than scores.
   Activities, and the rule that a Mini-app reports while the host judges. The tutor, and
   that opening it starts nothing.
2. **The sitting** (`mod-2`). Check, held, reveal, feedback, retake. The missed list, review
   at random, and the flag for a question that is broken rather than hard.
3. **Building** (`mod-3`). That the app spawns a CLI the reader installed themselves, the
   four stages of a build, and what costs money.

## Load-bearing points, and the misconceptions behind them

- **A Lesson records nothing.** The common misreading is that a Lesson is an easier Test.
  It is not a difficulty, it is what gets written down.
- **Results are held until the last question is checked.** Readers assume this is a bug or
  a delay. It is neither: knowing how question one went changes how they answer question two.
- **There is no score anywhere.** Not a percentage, not a mark on the reveal. Somebody
  looking for one should be told it does not exist rather than helped to find it.
- **A Mini-app never decides.** It reports what the reader did, and the comparison happens
  in the app, where the answer is kept.
- **Nothing spawns a model because they navigated somewhere.** Opening the tutor panel or a
  page costs nothing. A run starts on a press.

## Notation and words

Use the app's own words exactly: Page, Lesson, Test, Try, Task, Attempt, tick, Missed,
sitting, activity, harness. Do not introduce quiz, exam, score, grade, streak or deck.

## Where the reader is likely to be stuck

- No harness installed, so building fails at the first press. Everything else works: say so
  plainly rather than treating the app as broken.
- Expecting feedback after each question in a test, and reading the held result as a failure
  of the app.
