Whetstone · design round 2 · 2026-09-05

Sharper

You kept the stone-and-amber palette and rejected the shape of it. Round 1 was rounded cards, soft borders, and even weight everywhere, which is the default look of every product built this decade. These three keep the colours and throw that form language out.

Your note on option A "colour scheme of this is best but its too reminiscent of slop web apps - i want something sharper with more cutting edge design rather than this bubbly inoffensive big tech slop"

So four things change across all three, and only the fourth is up for debate.

  1. No rounded corners. Radius drops to zero. A 12px radius on every surface is the single strongest signal of the look you rejected.
  2. Structure through rules, not cards. Hairlines and hard frames instead of floating boxes with borders on all four sides.
  3. Real typographic contrast. A compressed grotesque for display set tight and large, against small monospace labels in caps. Round 1 set almost everything at one weight and one size.
  4. How far to push it. That is the choice below. Restraint, technical, or loud.

Each variant shows the same two screens with the same content, so what differs is only the design.

01

Press

Newspaper structure. Hairline rules divide everything, nothing floats. Compressed Archivo caps for titles, monospace for every label and number. Amber is used once per screen and never for decoration.

display Archivo 700, wdth 85, -3% tracking, uppercase ui Inter Tight labels IBM Plex Mono caps, +13% tracking radius 0 rules 1px hairline

The chain rule

Module 02 / 8 min / next up

I know this

When a function is built from other functions, its rate of change is the product of the rates at each layer. That is the whole idea, and every neural network trains on it.

Insight. Backpropagation is the chain rule applied in reverse order, once per layer, reusing what it already computed.

Why is the inner derivative 6x?
Because the inside is 3x². Its derivative is 3 · 2x = 6x. The chain rule multiplies that by the outer derivative.

Pick one

or describe a hybrid

My recommendation is 01 Press. You will spend most of your hours in this app reading long prose and answering questions, and Press is the only one of the three whose sharpness comes from structure rather than from decoration, so it stays sharp without competing with the text. Blueprint wins if you want the app to feel like an instrument you operate, and it is the better fit if the live constructor terminal ends up being a screen you enjoy watching. Slab wins if you want the app to have an opinion the moment you open it, and its cost is fatigue over a long session.
